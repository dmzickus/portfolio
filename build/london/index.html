Your 4-week game plan

At under 1000, games are decided by tactics, not theory. The London is perfect because the same setup works every game, so your thinking time goes to spotting threats. Spend about 20 minutes a day here, then play.

Week 1

Learn the setup cold

  • Study Setup, then switch to Drill until you get a clean run.
  • Play 10 rapid games (15+10) as White using only the setup.
  • After each game, find the first move where you left the setup and ask why.
Week 2

The dangerous edge cases

  • Learn the ...Qb6 line and the b2 blunder. These lose the most games.
  • Learn the Englund trap. Low-rated opponents play it a lot.
  • Learn ...Bf5 and ...Bd6.
Week 3

Other setups

  • King's Indian (...g6) and Dutch (...f5): learn the h3 + Bh2 pocket.
  • Middlegame Plan A (Ne5 + Qh3): practice the h7 checkmate pattern.
Week 4+

Plans and review

  • Middlegame Plans B and C. Endgame checklist.
  • Play Black against the London using Beat the London. It teaches you what your opponents will try.
  • Keep reviewing your own losses: was it the opening, or a missed tactic?

The 6 rules of the London

  1. Bishop out before e3. Play Bf4 on move 2, then e3. Otherwise the bishop is stuck behind your pawn.
  2. Build the triangle. Pawns on c3, d4, e3. Don't trade on c5 or d5 unless it wins something.
  3. Knights on f3 and d2. Never c3 (that's a pawn square) unless the line says so.
  4. When the black queen hits b6, look at b2. Answer with Qb3 or Qc1.
  5. Don't let your bishop get hunted. vs ...Bd6 play Bg3. vs ...e5 or ...Nh5 ideas, play h3 so it can hide on h2.
  6. Then attack the king. Ne5, Qf3-h3, Bd3 at h7. That's your middlegame.

Tactics to look for every move

  • Bxh7+ / Qxh7#: when the f6 knight leaves, h7 is often undefended.
  • Ne5 forks: from e5 your knight hits c6, d7, f7, g6.
  • Qb3: hits b7 when Black's c8 bishop has left.
  • Bb5 pin: pins a knight on c6 to the king when the e8 king hasn't castled.

Before every move, check

  1. What did their last move attack?
  2. Are any of my pieces undefended? (b2 especially)
  3. Do I have a check, capture or threat?

Endgame goals

London games often reach endgames with a solid pawn structure for you. These goals turn that into wins.

Keep your dark-squared bishop

Your Bf4/Bg3 bishop is the London's best piece. In the endgame, it's strongest when Black's pawns are stuck on dark squares. Avoid trading it for a knight unless you win material.

Activate your king

Once queens are off, march your king to the center (e2, d3). A king in the center is worth an extra piece in the endgame.

Rooks on open files

Put rooks on the c-file or the half-open h-file. A rook reaching the 7th rank (your opponent's second row) usually wins pawns.

Use the queenside majority

If Black traded ...cxd4 and you recaptured exd4, you often have more pawns on the queenside. Push them (b4, a4, b5) to make a passed pawn.

Trade when ahead

Up material? Trade pieces, not pawns. Every trade makes your extra material count more.

Know these basic wins

  • King + queen vs king checkmate
  • King + rook vs king checkmate
  • King + pawn vs king: the "opposition" rule

Practice them on lichess.org/practice until they're automatic.
